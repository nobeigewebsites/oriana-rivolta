A NOTE FROM ORIANA RIVOLTA

The story is fiction. The history is real.

Every story begins somewhere. Sometimes with a document. Sometimes with a discovery. And sometimes with a single question.

The First Thread is a work of historical fiction, but the world surrounding it is rooted in real history.

The manuscripts, archives, archaeological discoveries and historical practices explored here offer a glimpse into the inspirations, parallels and questions behind the story.

Below you will find links to genuine museums, libraries, historical collections and research resources.

These are not the fictional documents or objects from the novel. They are real places and surviving materials that illuminate the historical world and ideas explored within its pages.

Please feel free to explore further, follow whichever thread captures your curiosity and discover where history takes you.

You may find something unexpected. You may come away with more questions than answers.

And perhaps that is where the most interesting stories begin.

Oriana Rivolta

FLORENCE · NINETEENTH CENTURY

The City After the Crown

Florence, 1874. A city with a remarkable past and a rapidly changing future.

In The First Thread

Lucia Rossi's story begins in Florence in 1874, surrounded by historic streets, libraries, collections and changing ideas about the past.

The Real History

Florence was the capital of the Kingdom of Italy from 1865 to 1871. Its buildings, streets and institutions were shaped by significant nineteenth-century changes.

Can a city change its identity without losing its past?

HIDDEN WRITING · PALIMPSESTS

The Erased Record

Sometimes the most important words are the ones someone tried to remove.

In The First Thread

In 1548, Matteo Bellandi is instructed to alter a manuscript. Nine marks become eight, and an observation disappears from the page.

The Real History

Some historical manuscripts contain erased and overwritten writing. A famous example, the Archimedes Palimpsest, has revealed older texts beneath later writing.

What if the truth was never lost, only hidden beneath another story?

Explore a Real Erased Manuscript

Discover the Archimedes Palimpsest, the science used to examine it, and images of its surviving pages.

A NOTE ON HISTORY AND FICTION

Inspired by history. Created through imagination.

The characters, secret documents, nine-marked stone and central mystery of The First Thread belong to its fictional narrative.

The historical institutions, manuscripts, collections and archaeological materials linked throughout this page are real. They provide background, parallels and inspiration for exploring the ideas behind the novel.

Each link opens the original institution's own website. Some resources may be in Italian, and collections may contain further links to catalogues, images or digitised materials.

Feel free to explore further. Curiosity has no final chapter.
